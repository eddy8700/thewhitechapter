Practical Checklist

Getting these printed properly

Boutique menus fail on cheap paper, careless ink, and generic finish. A few small choices separate "just a menu" from "a keepsake." Here's what to insist on.

Uncoated paper only

No glossy laminated menus. Ask specifically for uncoated (matte) stock — it's warmer, ages better, and reads like a real book. Suggested brands in India: Sirio, Popset, Keaykolour.

Two-colour print (Pantone matched)

Get your printer to match the forest green and ember exactly (Pantone 5535 & Pantone 7523 approximately). Standard CMYK looks flat and muddy compared to spot colour.

Copper foil stamping

The peak-and-sprig crest on the cover of the restaurant booklet should be foil-stamped in copper (or bronze). ~₹3-4 per stamp at scale. Adds real luxury cost-effectively.

Blind emboss the logo

Optional, striking: a blind emboss (no ink, just the raised imprint) of the crest on the café card cover feels like a small secret. Guests always run their thumb over it.

Rounded or deckle corners

Straight corners feel corporate. Rounded (2mm radius) softens the object. Deckle-edge (torn) is more expensive but unforgettable — reserve for the restaurant booklet.

Print in Delhi or Chandigarh

Manali/Kullu printers can handle simple work, but for foil, emboss, and premium paper you need a metro press. Recommend: Impressions in Delhi, or Sunview in Chandigarh.

Order 10% more than you need

Menus get wine-stained, ripped, taken home. If you need 150 restaurant menus for the season, order 165. Reprints are expensive at low volume.

Update seasonally, not permanently

Print "Winter 2026" on the cover. That way you can reprint each season with new dishes without seeming stale. Guests love seeing a fresh dated menu.

Budget for both menus (150 copies each, premium print with foil + emboss): approximately ₹18,000 - ₹24,000 for the full print run per season. Per menu cost: café ~₹40, restaurant ~₹120. Recovered easily in a single meal.
